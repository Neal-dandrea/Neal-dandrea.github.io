02

Predicting an outcome from a variable-length sequence

Time-series sensor data, one series per experiment, and the series are not the same length. The target is a single outcome per series rather than a value per step, which rules out a lot of the obvious framings.

Padding, and the compute I was throwing away

The obvious way to batch variable-length series is to pad them all to the longest one. That works and it is wasteful, because most of the batch is then padding and the recurrent layer walks through all of it.

Packed sequences fixed that. The saving is not small when the length distribution is skewed, and it costs nothing in accuracy because the padding was never information in the first place.

The NaN losses

Training would run for a while and then the loss would become NaN, which is the least informative failure in the business. It was not one cause. It was a learning rate that was fine at the start and not after the scheduler moved, combined with an architecture that let activations grow without anything holding them.

What fixed it was dropout, a plateau scheduler on the learning rate, and systematic tuning across Adam and SGD rather than switching optimiser and hoping. I also started exporting to ONNX and reading the graph in Netron, because a computational graph you can look at answers questions that a training curve cannot.

03

Does this feature block help

The most useful model I run is a ridge regression, and it is not there to predict anything. It is there to answer whether a block of features carries information, cheaply enough that the answer arrives before anybody has built on the assumption that it does.

This is the panel I would point a reviewer at. The finding is negative and I think it is the best work on this page.

What the two blocks were, and why I expected them to work

One described the time of day and the position in the week as cyclical features. The other described the shape of a volatility surface, which was the only input in the set that said what the market expected rather than what it had already done. I built the second one specifically because the first failed, and I was confident about it in a way I am now suspicious of.

Both dropped out of sample. The honest reading is that roughly 3,100 daily samples against 13 existing features does not have room for more columns, and that adding them enlarges the surface to overfit against a signal nobody had yet shown exists.

The change that made every previous comparison suspect

Adding an embargo between the training and testing windows, so a feature with a fifty-bar lookback cannot see across the boundary. It made out-of-sample results worse on every symbol. On one name it fell by more than the gap between any two of the feature sets I had been comparing, which means those comparisons had been reading differences smaller than the bias they shared.

04

A policy when every action costs money

Reinforcement learning on a trading environment, with PPO and with DQN. The interesting part is not the algorithm. It is that the reward has to carry the cost of acting, and that an agent which is not charged for trading learns to trade constantly.

What convergence to buy and hold actually told me

My first reading was that the agent had failed. The better reading is that the environment did not contain an exploitable edge at the cost level it was charged, and the agent found the best available policy, which was to sit still.

That is a result about the data rather than about the algorithm, and it is the same conclusion the ablation reached from the other direction. Two methods agreeing on a negative is worth more than either one alone.

05

Adapting a language model on a small corpus

An open-weight model, a domain corpus far too small to justify touching every parameter, and a memory budget that rules out full fine tuning anyway. LoRA and QLoRA through the HuggingFace trainer, then post-training quantisation to get the result somewhere it can run.

Where quantisation stops being free

Compression is nearly free until it is suddenly not, and the point where it stops being free depends on the task rather than on the model. Judging it by perplexity alone missed it, because the number moved smoothly across exactly the range where the outputs on my actual task started degrading.

06

Answering questions over a document set

Retrieval-augmented generation with a vector store in Postgres. Most of the work here is not the generation. It is chunking, which sounds like a detail and decides almost everything downstream.

Measuring retrieval separately from the answer

For a while I evaluated the whole pipeline end to end and could not tell whether a bad answer meant bad retrieval or a model that had been given the right passage and used it badly. Separating them was the change that made the rest of the tuning possible, because they move in opposite directions as the chunk grows.

07

Physiological signals across settings that differ

Continuous glucose monitoring, in populations and environments the device was not originally designed around. The modelling question is less about accuracy on a benchmark and more about whether performance holds when the setting changes, which is the same question as the deployment gap on the robotics page wearing different clothes.

What I took from that work

That validation tooling is a deliverable rather than scaffolding. Being able to re-run the same evaluation across several physiological streams and several settings, and get a comparable answer each time, is what makes a claim about adaptability checkable instead of anecdotal.

08

What is next

Roughly the order I would pick it up.

  1. Sample size rather than architecture

    Everything on this page that returned a negative returned it for the same reason, which is that there was not enough independent data to support the model being asked for. Pooled training across symbols and longer histories is the lever. A different architecture on the same 3,100 samples is not.

  2. Evaluation that is decided in advance

    The embargo finding means I cannot fully trust comparisons I ran before it. Fixing that properly means the split, the embargo and the acceptance criterion written down before the run rather than chosen while looking at results.

  3. Retrieval quality as its own benchmark

    A small labelled set of questions with known correct passages, so retrieval can be scored without a language model in the loop at all.
