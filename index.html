Graduate Research Assistant

University of Cincinnati · Aug 2025 – present

Robot learning for assistive handover and in-space servicing, assembly and manufacturing — the problem of a robot putting an object into a person’s hand, reliably, outside the conditions it was trained in.

  • Building a control framework for robotic handover from imitation learning and vision–language–action models.
  • Migrated the manipulation stack from a 171M-parameter U-Net diffusion policy to NVIDIA GR00T N1.7, a 3B-parameter VLA, after diagnosing that the diffusion policy would not generalize outside its training distribution.
  • Built the conversion pipeline from Universal Manipulation Interface recordings to LeRobot v2.1 — absolute end-effector pose actions, language annotations and modality metadata across 265,000 frames and 215 demonstrations, with per-step validation that rejects SLAM tracking failures.
  • Deployed it as a real-time closed-loop controller: 125 ms GPU inference served over ZMQ, action chunks subsampled and rate-limited to a workspace-gated client, executed through Cartesian impedance control at 1 kHz on a Franka FR3.
  • Found and corrected systematic train–deploy mismatches: a 45-degree tool-frame rotation error, stale camera frames buffered across control cycles, and an observation mask that removed 5.6 percent of the image the model was trained on.
  • Traced a further source of domain shift to video compression in the data pipeline; rebuilt the conversion at 3× fidelity and retrained, cutting prediction error against the original model on 20 held-out episodes at every checkpoint — after first establishing that single-trajectory evaluation could not separate real improvement from sampler variance.
  • Built the collection pipeline itself: a UMI gripper with GoPro cameras and ArUco fiducials, video processed through ORB-SLAM3 to recover 6-DOF gripper poses synchronized with RGB observations and gripper state.
  • Exploring quantum-inspired low-rank attention to compress the vision encoder for resource-constrained space-grade hardware.

Quantitative Platform Engineer

EQ Risk Management Consulting · May 2026 – present

The full market data stack, from tick ingestion to backtest, now in daily production use by a trader with twenty years in institutional S&P 500 index options.

  • Architected and shipped an end-to-end platform in Python and C++ that ingests live equities, futures and options from the Schwab API over REST and WebSocket.
  • Built the historical store on KDB+/q with date-partitioned storage and a 500 ms tick capture pipeline across a multi-asset universe, on the order of tens of millions of rows per trading day.
  • Designed a bar aggregation engine for arbitrary intervals, including in-progress bars assembled from a rolling in-memory tick buffer — sub-minute resolution the vendor API does not expose.
  • Established the backtesting methodology: look-ahead bias eliminated through point-in-time sector and index membership reconstruction, survivorship correction, and embargoed chronological splits. Systematic ablation retired 13 of 14 candidate signal families as statistically indistinguishable from baseline.
  • Quantified strategy capacity through effective breadth — a nominal 126,756-bet universe delivered only 513 independent bets after correlation adjustment, which moved the research agenda off signal discovery and onto sample size.
  • Implemented a C++ compute kernel with Python orchestration and a GPU-accelerated backtest sweep, validating cross-implementation correctness through a 61-test differential parity harness.
  • Trained PPO agents with transaction-cost-aware reward shaping and calibrated slippage, evaluated against matched baselines on identical bars and cost assumptions.
